    Worksheet Section 3.5 - Subspaces of \(\real^p\)
    

    Definition 18.

    A subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^p\) is a subset of \(\mathbb{R}^p\) that is the span of a set of vectors.
    🔗
    🔗
    In this section we will look at some important subspaces.
    🔗

    1.

    Let’s start by understanding a few things about subspaces.
    🔗

    (a)

    If \(\vvec_1, \vvec_2, \dots, \vvec_n\) is a set of vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^m\text{,}\) explain why the zero vector \({\bf 0}\) can be expressed as a linear combination of these vectors. Usse this fact to explain why \({\bf 0}\) belongs to any subspace in \(\mathbb{R}^m\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Explain why the line on the left is not a subspace of \(\mathbb{R}^2\text{,}\) and the line on the right is.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Suppose that \(\vvec_1\text{,}\) \(\vvec_2\text{,}\) \(\vvec_3\text{,}\) and \(\vvec_4\) are four vectors in \(\real^3\) and that
    \begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} \vvec_1 \amp \vvec_2 \amp \vvec_3 \amp \vvec_4 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \amp -2 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \\ \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Give a description of the subspace \(S=\laspan{\vvec_1,\vvec_2,\vvec_3,\vvec_4}\) of \(\real^3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    The first important subspace we’ll look at is called the column space of \(A\) and denoted as \(\col(A)\text{.}\) It’s just the span of the columns of \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 20.

    If \(A\) is an \(m\times n\) matrix we call the span of its colums the column space of \(A\) and denote it as \(col(A)\text{.}\) That is
    \begin{equation*} \col(A) = \laspan{\vvec_1, \vvec_2, \vvec_3, \vvec_4}. \end{equation*}

    Definition 21.

    A basis for a subspace \(S\) of \(\mathbb{R}^p\) is a set of vectors in \(S\) that are linearly independent and whose span is \(S\text{.}\) We say that the dimension of the subspace \(S\text{,}\) denoted \(\dim S\) is the number of vectors in any basis.

    The second subspace we’ll look at is called the null space of \(A\) and is denoted \(\nul(A)\text{.}\) It’s just the solution set of the equation \(A\xvec = \zerovec\text{.}\)

    Definition 22.

    If \(A\) is an \(m\times n\) matrix we call the subset of vectors \(\xvec\) in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) satisfying \(A\xvec={\bf 0}\) the null space of \(A\) and denote it by \(\nul(A)\text{.}\)
