    Worksheet Section 2.3 - The Span of a Set of Vectors
    

    Definition 7.

    The span of a set of vectors \(\vvec_1, \vvec_2, \ldots, \vvec_n\) is the set of all linear combinations that can be formed from the vectors.
    🔗
    Alternatively, if \({A = \left[\vvec_1 \,\,\, \vvec_2 \,\,\, \cdots \,\,\, \vvec_n\right],}\) then the span of the vectors consists of all vectors \(\bvec\) for which the equation \(A\xvec = \bvec\) is consistent.
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    The setting: You are a young traveler, leaving home for the first time. Your parents want to help you on your journey, so just before your departure, they give you two gifts. Specifically, they give you two forms of transportation: a hoverboard and a magic carpet. Your parents inform you that the hoverboard and the magic carpet have restrictions in how they operate.
    🔗
    Hoverboard’s Restrictions: We denote the restriction on the hoverboard’s movement by the vector \(\vvec = \twovec{3}{1}\text{.}\) By this we mean that if the hoverboard traveled "forward" for one hour, it would move along a "diagonal" path that would result in a displacement of 3 miles East and 1 mile North of its starting location. You can also put it in reverse - after one hour you’d be 3 miles West and 1 mile South.
    🔗
    Magic Carpet’s Restrictions: We denote the restriction on the magic carpet’s movement by the vector \(\wvec = \twovec{1}{2}\text{.}\) By this we mean that if the magic carpet traveled "forward" for one hour, it would move along a "diagonal" path that would result in a displacement of 1 mile East and 2 miles North of its starting location. You can also take this in reverse as well.
    🔗

    (a)

    Use the tape to mark the origin and \(x\) and \(y\) axis. Each tile is one unit. You’ve made a "floor plane."
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Travel with the hoverboard for one hour, and then travel with the magic carpet forward for one hour. Where do you end up?
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Now do the opposite - travel with the magic carpet forward for one hour, and then travel with the hoverboard forward for one hour. Where do you end up? What does this say about \(\vvec+\wvec\) vs. \(\wvec+\vvec\text{?}\) Does this hold in general?
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Where do you end up if you travel in the reverse direction on your magic carpet for 1.5 hours?
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    You lost your magic carpet. Starting at the origin and using just your hoverboard, can you get to your friend’s house at \(\twovec{-6}{-2}\text{?}\) Can you get to the grocery store at \(\twovec{2}{4}\text{?}\) How would you describe the collection of places you can get to? (I.e., is it a point or a line or all of \(\mathbb{R}^2\text{?}\))
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    Good news, you’re back at the origin, and you have both your magic carpet and your hoverboard. You want to go visit the wise man, Old Man Gauss, who lives in a cabin that is 107 miles East and 64 miles North of your home. Is it possible to get there? If so, express how using a linear combination/vector notation. If not, explain why not. Is there more than one way to get there?
    🔗
    🔗

    (g)

    Old Man Gauss now tries to hide somewhere you can’t visit him. Is there somewhere he could hide? How would you describe a collection of places you can get to?
    🔗
    🔗

    (h)

    Your cousin also gets a magic carpet and a hoverboard from your aunt and uncle. His magic carpet also travels in the direction \(\vvec = \twovec{3}{1}\text{,}\) but his hoverboard travels in the direction \(\uvec = \twovec{6}{2}\text{.}\) Use your "floor plane" to determine where your cousin can go. Can Old Man Gauss hide from your friend?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let’s put some more technical terms to the magic carpet/hoverboard scenario. Recall the following movement:
    • You and your cousin’s hoverboard travel in the direction \(\vvec = \twovec{3}{1}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your magic carpet can move in the direction \(\wvec = \twovec{1}{2}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your cousin’s magic carpet can move in the direction \(\uvec = \twovec{6}{2}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    (a)

    Describe the set of vectors that result from linear combinations of \(\vvec\) and \(\wvec\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Describe the set of vectors that result from linear combinations of \(\vvec\) and \(\uvec\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What is Span \(\{\vvec,\wvec\}\text{?}\) Try to describe geometrically and algebraically.
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    What is Span \(\{\vvec,\uvec\}\text{?}\) Try to describe geometrically and algebraically.
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    For what vectors \(\bvec\) does the matrix equation \(\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 2 \\ \end{array} \right]\xvec = \bvec\) have a solution?
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    For what vectors \(\bvec\) does the matrix equation \(\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 3 \amp 6 \\ 1 \amp 2 \\ \end{array} \right]\xvec = \bvec\) have a solution?
    🔗
    🔗

    (g)

    How many pivots does the matrix \(\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 2 \\ \end{array} \right]\) have? How might this be related to your answer for Span \(\{\vvec,\wvec\}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The row reduced echelon form of \(\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 2 \\ \end{array} \right]\) is the identity matrix, which means that the system is always consistent, no matter what \(\bvec\) is. There is a pivot in every row!
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (h)

    How many pivots does the matrix \(\left[ \begin{array}{rr} 3 \amp 6 \\ 1 \amp 2 \\ \end{array} \right]\) have? How might this be related to your answer for Span \(\{\vvec,\uvec\}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Outcomes

      🔗
    • How can we determine if a vector is in the span of another set of vectors?
